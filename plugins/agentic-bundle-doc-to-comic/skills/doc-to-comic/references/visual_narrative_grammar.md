# 📐 Visual Narrative Grammar & Sequential Art Rules

This guide formalizes how to convert literary or academic text into panel sequences according to established sequential art theories.

---

## 1. Dr. Neil Cohn's Visual Narrative Grammar (VNG)

Comics sequences operate according to hierarchical syntax rather than linear, unlinked pictures:

| Role | Symbol | Description | Example (Scientific Document) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Establisher** | **$E$** | Sets the physical environment without state transition. | Wide shot of a university laboratory at night. |
| **Initial** | **$I$** | Commences the action or introduces a state of tension. | A researcher notices an anomaly on an oscilloscope. |
| **Prolongation** | **$L$** | Extends anticipation or delays the climax. | The researcher hesitates, checking the dials again. |
| **Peak** | **$P$** | The core transformation, dramatic climax, or focal event. | The particle laser fires and punctures the vacuum chamber. |
| **Release** | **$R$** | The resolution, reaction, or aftermath of the Peak. | The researcher drops their notebook in utter awe. |

### Rule: Mandatory Structure
Every sequence of 3 to 6 panels on a comic page **MUST** contain at least one **Initial ($I$)**, one **Peak ($P$)**, and one **Release ($R$)**. Never generate a series consisting purely of static establishers ($E-E-E$).

## 2. Scott McCloud's Panel-to-Panel Transitions

The transition chosen across the **gutter** (the white space between panels) controls reader participation and closure:

1. **Moment-to-Moment:** Minimal time increment (e.g., panel 1: eyes open; panel 2: eyes close). Use for intense suspense or slow-motion scientific reactions.
2. **Action-to-Action:** Single subject completing successive motions (e.g., panel 1: arm raises; panel 2: lever is pulled). The workhorse of narrative comics.
3. **Subject-to-Subject:** Stays in the same scene but cuts from speaker to listener or from actor to observer.
4. **Scene-to-Scene:** Massive spatial or temporal jump (e.g., panel 1: London 1888; panel 2: Tokyo 2088). Requires explanatory caption box.
5. **Aspect-to-Aspect:** Wandering focus capturing different angles, moods, or sensory elements of a frozen moment. **Vital for textbooks**: use this when explaining abstract mechanisms or ecosystems.
6. **Non-Sequitur:** Surreal, disconnected imagery. Use only for dream sequences, hallucinations, or symbolic metaphors.

## 3. Will Eisner's Page Staging & Eye Flow

* **The Z-Path Rule (Western Layout):** Eyes travel horizontally left-to-right, then sweep down and left again. 
* **Balloon Stacking:** The first speaker's balloon MUST be positioned higher than the replying speaker's balloon. Never place the response above the prompt.
* **Gutter Sizing:** Standard gutters should be 10–20px wide. Borderless panels or bleeds are reserved exclusively for dramatic peaks ($P$) or cosmic establishers ($E$).
