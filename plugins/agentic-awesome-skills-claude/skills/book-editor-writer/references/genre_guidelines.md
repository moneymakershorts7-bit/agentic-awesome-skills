# Genre Adaptation & Stylistic Guidelines

This reference document outlines specific editorial mechanics, prose rhythms, rhetorical strategies, and structural devices for each major book category.

---

## 1. Narrative, Fiction & True Stories (Creative Non-Fiction)

* **Guiding Persona:** Master Storyteller & Novelist.
* **Prose Rhythm:** Dynamic cadence; mix short, punchy sentences during high-tension scenes with rich, evocative descriptions during world-building.
* **Structural Elements:**
  * **Inciting Incident & Stakes:** Establish early what is at risk for the protagonists or subjects.
  * **Scene vs. Summary:** Dramatize pivotal moments as scenes with sensory details, dialogue, and spatial awareness; summarize transitions.
  * **Chapter Endings:** Conclude every chapter with an unresolved narrative beat, an unanswered question, or an emotional hook.
* **Formatting Conventions:**
  * Em-dashes (`—`) for dialogue and narrative pauses.
  * Italics for internal monologues or emphasis.

---

## 2. Technical, Research & Reference Manuals

* **Guiding Persona:** Senior Systems Architect & Technical Educator.
* **Prose Rhythm:** Crisp, disambiguated, highly didactic, and objective.
* **Structural Elements:**
  * **Progressive Disclosure:** Start from basic operational definitions before advancing to edge cases and architectural paradigms.
  * **Conceptual Analogies:** Ground abstract mechanisms in intuitive real-world metaphors.
  * **Actionable Breakdowns:** Convert multi-step processes into numbered execution sequences.
* **Formatting Conventions:**
  * Markdown tables for comparative matrices and parameter specifications.
  * Syntax-highlighted code/config blocks with line-by-line annotations where applicable.
  * Visual callouts (`> [!NOTE]`, `> [!IMPORTANT]`, `> [!CAUTION]`).

## 3. Essays, Academic Critique & Opinion

* **Guiding Persona:** Classical Rhetorician & Critical Essayist.
* **Prose Rhythm:** Elegant, logically sequenced, rhythmically balanced, and syntactically rigorous.
* **Structural Elements:**
  * **Dialectical Architecture:** Present thesis, deploy empirical proof, formulate the strongest version of the counter-argument (steelmanning), and resolve through synthesis.
  * **Epistemic Modesty:** Avoid unfounded universal claims; specify boundary conditions under which assertions hold.
  * **Intellectual Rigor:** Ground interpretations in primary philosophical, historical, or sociopolitical literature.
* **Formatting Conventions:**
  * Thematic subheadings.
  * Blockquotes for authoritative primary texts (`> 📜`).

---

## 4. Biographies & Memoirs

* **Guiding Persona:** Empathetic Historian & Literary Biographer.
* **Prose Rhythm:** Reflective, evocative, rich with historical context and character introspection.
* **Structural Elements:**
  * **Contextual Anchor:** Frame personal choices within the economic, technological, and cultural milieu of the era.
  * **Interiority vs. Action:** Balance what the subject accomplished with their inner doubts, motivations, and moral dilemmas.
  * **Legacy Assessment:** Conclude epochs by evaluating both the immediate fallout and long-term historical imprint.
* **Formatting Conventions:**
  * Excerpts from personal correspondence, journals, or oral histories in distinct typography.
  * Chronological markers and timelines at chapter boundaries.

---

## 5. Personal Development, Business & Strategy

* **Guiding Persona:** Executive Mentor & Growth Strategist.
* **Prose Rhythm:** Energetic, direct, pragmatic, urgent, and focused on reader action.
* **Structural Elements:**
  * **Problem-Framework-Action Loop:**
    1. Pain Point Diagnosis (the reader's struggle).
    2. Proprietary Framework or Mental Model (the conceptual breakthrough).
    3. Concrete Case Study (real-world validation).
    4. Implementation Drills (what to execute tomorrow morning).
  * **Cognitive Reinforcement:** Highlight key maxims in memorable epigrams.
* **Formatting Conventions:**
  * Bulleted Executive Summaries at the end of each chapter.
  * Checklist boxes and self-audit questionnaires (`- [ ]`).
  * Highlight boxes (`> 💡 **Principle:** ...`).

---

## 6. Popular Science & Investigative Journalism

* **Guiding Persona:** Investigative Journalist & Science Communicator (style of Malcolm Gladwell, Siddhartha Mukherjee, or Yuval Noah Harari).
* **Prose Rhythm:** Inquisitive, fast-paced, narrative-driven exploration that unpacks profound truths through small, captivating human stories.
* **Structural Elements:**
  * **The Micro-Case:** Open chapters with a specific, curious historical or laboratory vignette.
  * **The Broad Horizon:** Pivot from the singular anomaly to global patterns, scientific principles, and societal consequences.
  * **Ethical Dimension:** Explicitly address future ramifications, bioethics, algorithmic implications, or civilizational shifts.
* **Formatting Conventions:**
  * Anecdote-driven chapter titles and thematic interludes.
  * Inline explanatory notes for specialized terminology.
